## Project Background

Disney has released 573 movies spanning 1937 to 2016 across multiple genres from hand-drawn Musical classics to blockbuster Adventure franchises.

This was my first attempt at the **Onyx Data #dataDNA Challenge** . THis is a monthly
community data challenge for analytics practitioners. The goal was to identify
which genres have been truly profitable when inflation is considered, and what
release timing patterns drive maximum revenue.

## What I Did

I analyzed Disney's complete movie catalog using Power BI, examining genre
profitability, release trends and revenue patterns across eight decades.

The analysis covered four key areas:

- **Genre profitability** with and without inflation adjustment
- **Release timing patterns** by month and day of week
- **Year-over-year revenue trends** from 1937 to 2016
- **Top earning movies** by total and inflation-adjusted gross

## Key Findings

![Disney Movie Analysis Dashboard](/assets/img/portfolio/disney-dashboard.png)

- **Adventure** dominates total gross revenue at $24.6bn i.e. 43.75% of all revenue
- **Musical genre is the most profitable per movie** when inflation is applied - only 16 movies generating $9.7bn, with Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs alone earning $5.2bn inflation-adjusted
- Disney had maximum releases which is 113 movies between 1990 and 2000, experimenting with multiple genres after the revenue dip of the late 1980s
- **March and November** are peak release months . Spring Break and Holiday season drive maximum revenue
- **Fridays** are the preferred release day but 9 Tuesday releases made significant average gross, likely tied to pre-holiday timing
- Post-2000 Disney shifted strategy toward Adventure and Action, moving away from Musicals which once drove their highest per-movie returns
- **Comedies are underperforming** .182 movies but low average revenue per film
