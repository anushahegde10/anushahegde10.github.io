## The Business Question

Women first participated in the Olympics in 1900 with just 23 athletes. By 2016 that number had grown to 45.5% of all participants. The question was: **how has women's participation evolved across countries, sports and age groups — and what does this tell us about gender equity in international sport?**

## What I Did

Analyzed the complete Olympic dataset spanning 1900 to 2016, focusing specifically on the progressive change in women's participation across Summer and Winter games.

Key analysis areas:

- Female participation percentage trend from 1900 to 2016
- Country representation growth over time
- Top sports by female athlete participation
- Age distribution of female athletes
- Medal performance by country and athlete

## Key Findings

- Female participation grew from **0.6% in 1906 to 45.5% in 2016** — a 7,062% increase
- In 1900 only **6 countries** had female athletes. By 2016 that had grown to **200 countries** in Summer Olympics
- **Athletics** is the most participated sport with 11,666 female athletes, followed by Swimming and Gymnastics
- **USA** leads in total female performances with 5,533, followed by Germany and Russia
- Most female athletes are aged **21-25** — the same age group that wins the most medals
- **69%** of athletes have participated in only one Olympics — showing how competitive qualification is
- Youngest medal winner — Luigina Giavotti from Italy won Silver in Gymnastics in 1928 at just 11 years old

## Tools & Approach

- **Power BI** — interactive dashboard tracking participation and medal trends

- **Data Analysis** — trend analysis across 116 years of Olympic history
