## Project Background

Women first participated in the Olympics in 1900 with just 23 athletes from
6 countries. By 2016 that number had grown to 45.5% of all participants across
200 countries.

The Olympic dataset contains the complete history of all athletes across all
games. I chose to focus on a specific analytical question that mattered to me:
**how has women's participation evolved across countries, sports and age groups
and what does this tell us about gender equity in international sport?**

This was inspired by the terrific performance of women athletes at Tokyo
Olympics 2020.

## What I Did

I analyzed the complete Olympic dataset spanning 1900 to 2016, focusing
specifically on the progressive change in women's participation across Summer
and Winter games.

The analysis covered five key areas:

- **Female participation percentage** trend from 1900 to 2016
- **Country representation** growth over time
- **Top sports** by female athlete participation
- **Age distribution** of female athletes
- **Medal performance** by country and individual athlete

## Key Findings

![Olympics Women Dashboard](/assets/img/portfolio/olympics-dashboard.png)

- Female participation grew from **0.6% in 1906 to 45.5% in 2016** which is
  a 7,062% increase over 110 years
- In 1900 only **6 countries** had female athletes. By 2016 that had grown
  to **200 countries** in Summer Olympics alone
- **33,981 female athletes** from 207 countries have participated across
  53 sport events in total
- **Athletics** is the most participated sport with 11,666 female athletes,
  followed by Swimming and Gymnastics
- **Alpine Skiing** is the most preferred Winter sport
- **USA** leads in total female performances with 5,533, followed by
  Germany and Russia
- Most female athletes are aged **21-25** . The same age group that wins
  the most medals
- **69%** of athletes have participated in only one Olympics showing how
  competitive qualification truly is
- Youngest medal winner is Luigina Giavotti from Italy won Silver in
  Gymnastics in 1928 at just **11 years old**
- Larsya Semenivna Latynina from Russia holds the record with
  **9 Gold, 5 Silver and 4 Bronze** medals in Gymnastics
